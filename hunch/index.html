Hunch Awareness
Hunches are an invasive species affecting North America as you read this text. They must be terminated immediately.

Hunches (Pan arnesscon) are an aggressive primate species affecting North America as the current seconds tick by. They are characterized by their humanoid body with dark, oily, hairless skin, and always hunched over on all fours. They have feet identical to humans but have no hands, as they have another two set of feet in place where their hands would be. They have 2 small holes in place of their nose and their eyelids are almost always closed, along with their mouth. They react aggressively to any life they see and need to be terminated as soon as possible to prevent spread. Research indicates they are normally docile, and are highly attracted to light. They express curiosity and may approach areas where they should not be.

Since 2000, almost 60 people have died as a result of hunch attacks.

If you catch sight of a hunch, turn off all lights and evacuate the area immediately and do not approach it under any circumstances. Do not use a vehicle. If you are able to, kill it immediately with a ranged weapon.


The following images have not been seen by the public until now.

The most notable hunch attack happened in February 2003 in a trailer park. A woman, whose identity will remain undisclosed, noticed a hunch about 60 yards away on another property. It initially was seen outside her window emerging from the outside forest after being attracted by the light, however the light did not illuminate the woman. At approximately 9:00 P.M., the woman stepped out of her trailer as quietly as possible and took a photo of the hunch while it was looking in her direction. It is visible next to the large tree in front of one of the trailers, illuminated by the light. This is one of the only verifiable photos of a hunch. The woman retreated back inside and hid inside an enclosed room. The hunch stayed around the trailer park for around 6 hours before retreating back into the woods.

The hunch did not notice her from 60 yards.

The very next day, the hunch returned to the area at about 8:00 P.M. This night, many neighbors had left their porch lights on, attracting the hunch further into the trailer park. However, only the woman had left the lights inside her trailer on, particularly the bathroom lights, which prompted the hunch to enter. It is unknown whether she was asleep or awake, but she was inside the room she hid in the previous day and likely heard the hunch outside, because she once again took out a camera (albeit a different one) and took a photo of the bedroom door as the hunch broke into the trailer. At about 9:20 P.M., the hunch successfully broke into her trailer and quickly broke into her bedroom and violently mauled her to death. Her body was not found until the nest day during the afternoon, lying on her bed. The hunch was quickly hunted, found, and shot dead by wildlife control. Her body was in extensive state of disfigurement.
